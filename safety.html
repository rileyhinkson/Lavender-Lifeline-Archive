Travel Safety

LGBTQ+ & Trans Travel Safety

Legal and safety information by U.S. state and country, sourced from the Movement Advancement Project, ILGA World, and the U.S. State Department.

Informational purposes only. This page does not constitute legal or travel advice. Laws and enforcement conditions change rapidly — always verify current information before traveling. In an emergency abroad, contact your country's nearest embassy or consulate. Data reflects 2024 reporting.
Sources: Movement Advancement Project · ILGA World · Equaldex · U.S. State Department · Verify current status before travel.
United States

By State

State-level legal protections for LGBTQ+ and transgender people vary significantly across the United States. The categories below reflect the general policy environment as assessed by the Movement Advancement Project (MAP), which tracks non-discrimination protections in employment, housing, and public accommodations, as well as healthcare access and youth policies. Laws change — verify current status with MAP before traveling.

Note that local ordinances in some cities and counties may offer stronger protections than the surrounding state. The Bostock v. Clayton County (2020) Supreme Court decision extended federal employment non-discrimination protections to LGBTQ+ workers in all states under Title VII, though gaps in housing, healthcare, and public accommodations remain in many jurisdictions.
