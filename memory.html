In Memoriam

We remember those lost to hatred, violence, legal persecution, and systemic harm — because of who they were, who they loved, and how they lived. Their names endure.

This page is a living memorial. It will never be finished.
Names and details sourced from public records, the Transgender Day of Remembrance database, the Human Rights Campaign, and historical documentation. If someone is missing, reach out or open an issue on GitHub.
Historical Persecution & Legal Violence

Lost to the Law

People whose lives were destroyed or ended by governments and legal systems that criminalized who they were. Many were brilliant, defiant, and irreplaceable.

Alan Turing
1912 – 1954  ·  Age 41
British mathematician and WWII codebreaker. Convicted of "gross indecency" for homosexuality in 1952. Subjected to chemical castration by court order. Died of cyanide poisoning in 1954. Widely regarded as a persecution-related death. Posthumously pardoned in 2013.
Oscar Wilde
1854 – 1900  ·  Age 46
Irish playwright and poet. Imprisoned 1895–1897 for "gross indecency" for homosexuality. Subjected to hard labor which destroyed his health. Released destitute and in failing health. Died in Paris in 1900. "To deny one's own experiences is to put a lie into the lips of one's own life."
Harvey Milk
1930 – 1978  ·  Age 48
First openly gay person elected to public office in California. San Francisco City Supervisor. Assassinated on November 27, 1978, by former supervisor Dan White. His death sparked the White Night riots when White received a manslaughter sentence.
Federico García Lorca
1898 – 1936  ·  Age 38
Spanish poet and playwright. Executed by Nationalist forces at the start of the Spanish Civil War in August 1936. His homosexuality was among the reasons used to justify his murder. His body was never recovered.
Marsha P. Johnson
1945 – 1992  ·  Age 46
Black trans woman, LGBTQ+ activist, and prominent figure in the 1969 Stonewall uprising. Co-founder of STAR (Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries). Found drowned in the Hudson River on July 6, 1992. Death initially ruled suicide; later changed to undetermined. Many in the community believe she was murdered.
Roger Casement
1864 – 1916  ·  Age 51
Irish human rights activist and diplomat who documented atrocities in the Congo and Peru. Executed for treason in 1916. His private diaries documenting his homosexuality were leaked by the British government to discredit his cause and prevent clemency appeals.
The Holocaust  ·  1933–1945

🔺 Lost to the Pink Triangle

Under Nazi rule, gay men were forced to wear a downward-pointing pink triangle in concentration camps — a badge of persecution that later became a symbol of LGBTQ+ resistance and remembrance. The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum estimates that 100,000 gay men were arrested under Paragraph 175, and between 5,000 and 15,000 were sent to concentration camps — approximately 60 percent of whom were killed. Lesbian women were designated "asocials" and forced to wear a black triangle. Because families concealed their relatives' pink triangle status and records were systematically destroyed, the vast majority of those murdered were never documented by name. The names below represent the few who were identified — and the countless thousands who were not.

Josef Kohout
1915 – 1994  ·  Survived
Austrian gay man arrested in 1939 after love letters to a male classmate were discovered. Imprisoned in Flossenbürg and Mauthausen concentration camps. Published his testimony in 1972 under the pen name Heinz Heger as The Men with the Pink Triangle — one of the earliest and most important firsthand accounts of persecution of gay men in the camps. He wrote so others would know what was done in the dark.
Pierre Seel
1923 – 2005  ·  Survived
French gay man arrested in Strasbourg in 1941 at age 17. Sent to Schirmeck concentration camp. Witnessed the public execution of his lover, known only as "Jo," who was thrown to German Shepherd dogs while guards forced other prisoners to watch. After the war, Seel remained silent for decades out of shame and fear. He eventually published his memoir, I, Pierre Seel, Deported Homosexual (1994), becoming one of the few survivors to publicly testify. Jo's full name was never recorded.
Rudolf Brazda
1913 – 2011  ·  Survived
Czech-born gay man and the last known surviving gay Holocaust victim when he died at age 98. Arrested twice under Paragraph 175 and ultimately sent to Buchenwald in 1942. He survived the camp and lived quietly in France for decades before telling his story. In 2008, he laid a wreath at the Berlin Memorial to Homosexuals Persecuted under Nazism — an act of bearing witness no one else was left to make.
Karl Gorath
1912 – 2003  ·  Survived
German gay man arrested multiple times beginning in 1937 under Paragraph 175. Imprisoned in multiple concentration camps including Neuengamme, where he endured years of forced labor. Survived the war but was denied compensation by post-war German authorities because homosexuality remained criminalized in West Germany until 1969. He later testified publicly about his persecution and the suffering of gay prisoners in the camps.
"Jo" (name unknown)
– c. 1941
The lover of Pierre Seel, known only by his first name. Publicly executed at Schirmeck concentration camp in 1941 — thrown alive to German Shepherd dogs while prisoners were forced to watch — as a lesson in what awaited gay prisoners. His full name, age, and origin were never recorded. He exists in history only through Seel's memoir. He stands here for all the unnamed.
The Unnamed Thousands
1933 – 1945
Between 5,000 and 15,000 gay men died in Nazi concentration camps. Their names were not preserved. Families erased the pink triangle from their histories out of shame and fear. Post-war governments in both East and West Germany refused to recognize gay Holocaust survivors as victims, denied them compensation, and in some cases imprisoned them again under the same law used by the Nazis. The U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum maintains documentation of what is known.

Sources: U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum · Facing History & Ourselves — Paragraph 175 · Seel, Pierre. I, Pierre Seel, Deported Homosexual (1994) · Heger, Heinz. The Men with the Pink Triangle (1972).

The AIDS Epidemic

Lost to a Crisis Met with Silence

The AIDS epidemic killed hundreds of thousands of gay and queer people in the United States alone — many abandoned by their government, their families, and their institutions. These are some of the artists, activists, and individuals whose names we carry. The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt holds over 110,000 names.

Freddie Mercury
1946 – 1991  ·  Age 45
Musician and lead vocalist of Queen. Died of bronchopneumonia as a complication of AIDS on November 24, 1991 — one day after publicly announcing his diagnosis.
Rock Hudson
1925 – 1985  ·  Age 59
Hollywood actor. The first major celebrity to publicly announce he was dying of AIDS, in July 1985. His death helped push Congress toward increased AIDS research funding.
Keith Haring
1958 – 1990  ·  Age 31
Artist and LGBTQ+ activist. Founded the Keith Haring Foundation in 1989 to support AIDS organizations and children's programs. Died of AIDS-related complications on February 16, 1990.
Robert Mapplethorpe
1946 – 1989  ·  Age 42
Photographer known for his intimate and controversial work. Died of AIDS-related complications on March 9, 1989. His work continued to be censored and fought over long after his death.
Sylvester James
1947 – 1988  ·  Age 41
Openly gay Black disco and R&B singer known as "The Queen of Disco." His falsetto voice and fearless presence made him an icon. Died of AIDS-related heart failure on December 16, 1988. He left his royalties to AIDS charities.
Halston (Roy Frowick)
1932 – 1990  ·  Age 57
American fashion designer who defined 1970s American style. Died of AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma on March 26, 1990, in San Francisco.
Perry Ellis
1940 – 1986  ·  Age 46
American fashion designer. Died on May 30, 1986. His cause of death was listed as viral encephalitis; AIDS was not publicly disclosed at the time but is widely acknowledged.
Arthur Russell
1951 – 1992  ·  Age 40
Musician and composer whose work spanned disco, avant-garde, and folk. Died of AIDS-related complications on April 4, 1992. His music was largely rediscovered and celebrated after his death.
David Wojnarowicz
1954 – 1992  ·  Age 37
Artist, writer, and AIDS activist. A fierce voice against government inaction during the epidemic. Died of AIDS-related complications on July 22, 1992. His words: "If I die of AIDS, forget burial — just drop my body on the steps of the FDA."
Essex Hemphill
1957 – 1995  ·  Age 38
Black gay poet and activist. Editor of "Brother to Brother: New Writings by Black Gay Men." Died of AIDS-related complications on November 4, 1995.
Paul Monette
1945 – 1995  ·  Age 49
Poet and memoirist. Author of "Borrowed Time: An AIDS Memoir" and "Becoming a Man," which won the National Book Award. Died of AIDS-related complications on February 10, 1995.
Cookie Mueller
1949 – 1989  ·  Age 40
Writer, actress, and downtown New York fixture who appeared in many of John Waters' films. Died of AIDS-related complications on November 10, 1989.
Hate Crimes & Violence

Murdered for Who They Were

People killed because of their sexual orientation or gender identity. These are documented hate crimes and targeted killings. They represent thousands more tracked by the Transgender Day of Remembrance and the Human Rights Campaign.

Matthew Shepard
1976 – 1998  ·  Age 21
Gay student at the University of Wyoming. Tied to a fence post and beaten by two men in Laramie, Wyoming, on October 7, 1998. Died on October 12, 1998. His death led to the federal Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act, signed in 2009.
Brandon Teena
1972 – 1993  ·  Age 21
Trans man. Raped and then murdered in Falls City, Nebraska on December 31, 1993 by two men after his trans identity was revealed. His story is told in the film "Boys Don't Cry" (1999).
Gwen Araujo
1985 – 2002  ·  Age 17
Trans Latina woman. Murdered by four men in Newark, California on October 3, 2002 after they discovered she was trans. Her murder prompted California to pass legislation limiting the use of the trans panic defense.
Sakia Gunn
1987 – 2003  ·  Age 15
Black lesbian teenager. Stabbed while waiting for a bus with friends in Newark, New Jersey on May 11, 2003, after identifying herself as gay when two men made advances. One of the youngest known victims of an anti-LGBTQ+ hate crime.
Lawrence "Larry" King
1993 – 2008  ·  Age 15
Gay and gender nonconforming student at E.O. Green Junior High School in Oxnard, California. Shot twice in the back of the head by a 14-year-old classmate on February 12, 2008. He died two days later.
Angie Zapata
1989 – 2008  ·  Age 18
Trans woman. Murdered in Greeley, Colorado in July 2008. Her killer became the first person in the United States convicted under a hate crime law for killing a transgender person.
Duanna Johnson
1965 – 2008  ·  Age 43
Black trans woman. Beaten by Memphis police officers in a booking room in February 2008 — a beating caught on video. Shot and killed in November 2008. Her case illustrated the compounding vulnerability of trans women of color to both police violence and street violence.
Jorge Steven Lopez Mercado
1992 – 2009  ·  Age 17
Gay Puerto Rican youth. Murdered near Cayey, Puerto Rico in November 2009. His body was dismembered and partially burned. His death prompted widespread protests demanding justice for LGBTQ+ Puerto Ricans.
Mark Carson
1981 – 2013  ·  Age 32
Black gay man. Shot point-blank in Greenwich Village, New York City on May 18, 2013 by a man who had been following and taunting him and his partner with anti-gay slurs. His murder galvanized the New York LGBTQ+ community.
Islan Nettles
1993 – 2013  ·  Age 21
Black trans woman. Beaten to death in Harlem, New York in August 2013. Her killer discovered she was trans and beat her while bystanders watched. Her case became a rallying point for justice for trans women of color.
Brianna Ghey
2006 – 2023  ·  Age 16
Trans girl from Birchwood, Warrington, England. Murdered on February 11, 2023, in a planned attack by two teenagers. Her mother, Esther Ghey, became a prominent advocate for online safety and anti-knife legislation.
Nex Benedict
2007 – 2024  ·  Age 16
Nonbinary (they/he) student in Owasso, Oklahoma. Beaten in a school bathroom by multiple students on February 7, 2024. Died the following day. Their death drew national attention to anti-LGBTQ+ legislation targeting youth in Oklahoma and across the country.
Transgender Day of Remembrance — Selected Names

Trans Lives Lost to Violence

The Transgender Day of Remembrance (TDOR), founded in 1999 by Gwendolyn Ann Smith, memorializes trans people killed due to anti-transgender violence. Trans women of color are disproportionately represented among victims. The names below are drawn from HRC's annual fatal violence tracking. The full TDOR list documents hundreds per year — thousands since 1999.

Lamia Beard
~1984 – January 17, 2015  ·  Age 30
Black trans woman. Shot at 4 a.m. on January 17, 2015 in Norfolk, Virginia, and found on a sidewalk by police responding to a 911 call. Died of her injuries at a local hospital. The National Coalition of Anti-Violence Programs identified her as the first trans person of color murdered in 2015. Media outlets repeatedly misgendered her in coverage of her death.
Ty Underwood
~1991 – January 26, 2015  ·  Age 24
Black trans woman. Shot and killed in North Tyler, Texas on January 26, 2015. Her boyfriend, Carlton Ray Champion Jr. — a student at Texas College — was convicted of her murder after evidence showed he had driven to meet her for a date. A jury deliberated for only 45 minutes before sentencing him to life in prison and a $10,000 fine.
Taja Gabrielle DeJesus
~1979 – February 1, 2015  ·  Age 36
Trans Latina woman. Found stabbed multiple times in a stairwell in San Francisco's Bayview District on the morning of February 1, 2015. An active member of a local church, a food pantry volunteer, and a volunteer with Trans Thrive. The suspected killer, James Hayes, was found dead in an apparent suicide shortly after her body was discovered. She was the fourth trans woman of color murdered in 2015.
Penny Proud
~1994 – February 10, 2015  ·  Age 21
Black trans woman. Fatally shot multiple times on February 10, 2015, at 1:30 a.m. on North Claiborne Avenue in the Tremé district of New Orleans during a robbery. The fifth trans woman of color murdered in the U.S. in 2015. Local LGBTQ+ groups condemned media misgendering. A billboard featuring her image was later erected in New Orleans to raise awareness of anti-trans violence.
Bri Golec
~1993 – February 13, 2015  ·  Age 22
Gender nonconforming and trans-identified person. Stabbed to death in Akron, Ohio on February 13, 2015 by her father, Kevin Golec, 52. He called 911 falsely claiming a cult was responsible before police determined he had committed the killing. He was charged with murder and felony domestic violence. Bri was described by local advocates as having been actively exploring her gender identity in the period before her death.
London Kiki Chanel
~1994 – May 18, 2015  ·  Age 21
Black trans woman. Stabbed twice in the back and once in the neck inside an abandoned rowhome in North Philadelphia on May 18, 2015. She had moved to Philadelphia from her hometown of Victoria, Texas. Raheam Felton, 31, was arrested immediately after the attack, pleaded guilty to murder, and was sentenced to 22 to 44 years in prison in 2016.
India Clarke
~1990 – July 21, 2015  ·  Age 25
Black trans woman. Found beaten and shot near the basketball courts at the University Area Community Center in Tampa, Florida on July 21, 2015. A park employee discovered her body just before 9 a.m. Keith Gaillard, 18, was charged with first-degree murder. Law enforcement and media repeatedly misgendered her after her death. She was the tenth trans woman murdered in the U.S. in 2015.
Shade Schuler
~1993 – July 29, 2015  ·  Age 22
Black trans woman. Shot to death on July 29, 2015 in Dallas, Texas. Her badly decomposed body was found in a vacant field two weeks later. Police initially misidentified her gender. Trans Pride Initiative's Nell Gaither worked to confirm her identity with police and media. Her murder was the 13th reported killing of a trans person in the U.S. in 2015 — pushing the year's toll past the total for all of 2014.
Ashton O'Hara
~1990 – July 14, 2015  ·  Age 25
Black trans and genderfluid person, also known as Jessica Storm. Body discovered in a field in Detroit, Michigan on July 14, 2015. Ashton loved dancing, performing, and doing hair — teaching himself to braid extensions at age four. He was devoted to children and to his community. Larry B. Gaulding was sentenced to 30–60 years for voluntary manslaughter and 10 years for evidence tampering.
Amber Monroe
~1995 – August 2015  ·  Age 20
Black trans woman and student at Wayne State University in Detroit. Shot and killed on Woodward Avenue in Detroit in August 2015. Detroit police refused to confirm her name to media because her legal ID listed her as male — denying her identity in death as well. She was the twelfth trans woman murdered in the U.S. in 2015.
Kandis Capri
~1980 – August 11, 2015  ·  Age 35
Black trans woman. Shot multiple times in a parking lot near 45th Avenue and Thomas Road in Phoenix, Arizona on August 11, 2015. Her purse and phone were stolen. She had been taking classes at South Mountain Community College and was described as kind with a vivacious personality. A surveillance video from a nearby convenience store captured her final moments; the case went cold despite a public video release by police.
K.C. Haggard
~1949 – July 23, 2015  ·  Age 66
Trans woman. Stabbed in the throat at 1:43 a.m. on July 23, 2015 at Blackstone Avenue and Cornell Avenue in Fresno, California. A retired Chapman University graduate who had worked as a security guard and legal marketer. Richard Lopez, 38, was arrested three years later and sentenced to 23 years in prison for her murder.
Tamara Dominguez
~1979 – August 17, 2015  ·  Age 36
Trans Latina woman. Run over by a black Chevrolet SUV at 3 a.m. on August 15, 2015 in Kansas City, Missouri. Witnesses saw her exit the vehicle before the driver struck her and ran over her body at least two more times. She died in hospital two days later. Luis M. Sanchez, 29, was charged with first-degree murder. Missouri's hate crime statute was not applied.
Elisha Walker
~1995 – 2014/2015  ·  Age ~20
Black trans woman. Reported missing by her mother in November 2014. Her decomposed remains were found in a shallow grave in Johnston County near Smithfield, North Carolina in August 2015. Her burned car had been found in Sampson County. Angel Arias, 25, a Latin Kings gang member, pleaded guilty to second-degree murder and was sentenced to 19 to 24 years in prison.
Keisha Jenkins
~1993 – October 6, 2015  ·  Age 22
Black trans woman and Philadelphia native who attended West Philadelphia High School and Temple University. Beaten by a group of five to six men and then shot in the back at 13th and Wingohocking Streets in North Philadelphia in the early hours of October 6, 2015. Pedro Redding, 24, was arrested and charged with murder. She was the twentieth trans woman reported murdered in the U.S. in 2015.
Zella Ziona
~1994 – October 15, 2015  ·  Age 21
Black trans woman. Shot in the head in an alley behind a shopping mall in Gaithersburg, Maryland on October 15, 2015. Rico LeBlond, a childhood friend, was ultimately sentenced to 35 years in prison after a complex legal case involving two trials, a mistrial, an overturned conviction, and a final guilty plea. She was the twenty-first trans woman reported murdered in the U.S. in 2015.
Jaquarrius Holland
~1998 – February 19, 2017  ·  Age 18
Black trans woman. Shot and killed in Monroe, Louisiana on February 19, 2017. Her death was the seventh reported homicide of a trans woman of color in the first two months of 2017 — a period that saw four trans women killed in Louisiana alone within a single week. Local media repeatedly misgendered her. The NCAVP condemned the pace of killings as an alarming trend.
Mesha Caldwell
– January 4, 2017
Black trans woman. Murdered in Canton, Mississippi on January 4, 2017. Her death opened a year in which at least 28 trans people were killed in the United States — the highest recorded total at the time. She was among the first trans people killed in 2017 and one of four Black trans women murdered in a single week in late February.
Chyna Gibson
– February 25, 2017
Black trans woman. Shot to death outside a shopping center in New Orleans, Louisiana on February 25, 2017. Neighbors reported hearing ten gunshots. Her murder was one of three trans women killed within a single week in Louisiana in late February 2017, prompting advocacy groups to call the situation a state of emergency.
Ciara McElveen
– February 27, 2017
Black trans woman. Stabbed multiple times in New Orleans, Louisiana on February 27, 2017. She was dragged from a car by the driver before being attacked. The third trans woman killed in Louisiana within a week, her death intensified national attention on the epidemic of anti-trans violence in the South.
Muhlaysia Booker
– May 18, 2019
Black trans woman. In April 2019, she survived a brutal mob attack in a parking lot in Dallas, Texas that was captured on video and shared widely — drawing national outrage. Despite surviving, she was shot and killed on May 18, 2019, just weeks later. Her case drew national attention to the compounding dangers faced by Black trans women and the failure of authorities to protect them.
Dana Martin
~1988 – January 6, 2019  ·  Age 31
Black trans woman. Found shot to death in her car, which had crashed into a ditch, in Montgomery County, Alabama on January 6, 2019. She was the first known trans person killed in the United States in 2019. Law enforcement and media initially misgendered her, delaying public recognition of her death. No arrest was immediately reported.
Monika Diamond
~1986 – March 18, 2020  ·  Age 34
Black trans woman, community leader, and business owner in Charlotte, North Carolina. Co-founder of Ncphyne Promotion Company and co-CEO of the International Mother of the Year Pageantry System. Shot to death on March 18, 2020, after being placed in an ambulance. Prentice Bess, 34, was arrested and charged with murder. She was active in Charlotte's LGBTQ+ nightlife and advocacy community.
Dominique "Rem'mie" Fells
– June 2020
Black trans woman. Murdered in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania in June 2020. Her death occurred within days of the killing of Riah Milton and during the height of Black Lives Matter protests across the country — a painful convergence that underscored how violence against Black trans women continued even as the nation reckoned with systemic racism.
Riah Milton
– June 2020
Black trans woman. Murdered in Liberty Township, Ohio in June 2020. Her killing and that of Dominique Fells, occurring within the same days, prompted a national outcry and vigils across the country. The Human Rights Campaign noted that 2020 was on pace to be the deadliest recorded year for trans people in the United States.
Neulisa Luciano Ruiz (Alexa)
– February 24, 2020
Trans Latina woman. Murdered in Toa Baja, Puerto Rico on February 24, 2020. Video of her murder was filmed and circulated on social media by bystanders, forcing police to act. Her case drew widespread condemnation and highlighted the extreme vulnerability of trans women in Puerto Rico. Her preferred name was Alexa.
Juniper Blessing
~2006 – May 10, 2026  ·  Age 19
Trans woman (she/they). University of Washington student studying Atmospheric and Climate Science with planned minors in Music and Philosophy. A gifted singer and graduate of the New Mexico School for the Arts in Santa Fe; loved meteorology, music, and video games. Stabbed more than 40 times in the laundry room of the Nordheim Court Apartments near the UW campus on the night of May 10, 2026. Christopher Michael Leahy, 31, surrendered to Bellevue police and was charged with first-degree murder with a deadly weapon; bail was set at $10 million. Her family said: "Juniper was simply the most amazing human being we ever knew — highly intelligent, extremely talented, and deeply sensitive to others' needs." A memorial grew in UW's Red Square in the days after her death.

This represents a small portion of documented cases. The Transgender Day of Remembrance database and HRC's annual tracking document hundreds of additional names each year. Source: Human Rights Campaign Fatal Violence Reports, 2015–2024.

The Full Record

The names above represent a small portion of those documented. These organizations maintain comprehensive, ongoing records — together, they hold thousands of names and the evidence of a systemic crisis.

↗
Transgender Day of Remembrance
Founded 1999 by Gwendolyn Ann Smith. Documents trans people killed due to anti-trans violence worldwide, annually. Hundreds of names per year — thousands total.
 ↗
HRC — Fatal Anti-Transgender Violence
Annual Human Rights Campaign report documenting fatal violence against trans and gender nonconforming people in the United States, with individual names and circumstances.
 ↗
NAMES Project — AIDS Memorial Quilt
Over 110,000 names of people lost to AIDS/HIV, displayed on hand-stitched panels. The largest piece of community folk art in the world. Searchable database available online.
 ↗
Anti-Violence Project
Documents and responds to violence against LGBTQ+ and HIV-affected communities. Maintains incident data and provides direct services to survivors.
 ↗
GLAAD — TDOR Resources
Annual Transgender Day of Remembrance resources and coverage, including context on the epidemic of anti-trans violence.
 ↗
Matthew Shepard Foundation
Founded by Dennis and Judy Shepard after the murder of their son Matthew in 1998. Works to replace hate with understanding, compassion, and acceptance.
