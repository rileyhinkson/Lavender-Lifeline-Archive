In Memoriam

We remember those lost to hatred, violence, legal persecution, and systemic harm — because of who they were, who they loved, and how they lived. Their names endure.

This page is a living memorial. It will never be finished.
Names and details sourced from public records, the Transgender Day of Remembrance database, the Human Rights Campaign, and historical documentation. If someone is missing, reach out or open an issue on GitHub.
Historical Persecution & Legal Violence

Lost to the Law

People whose lives were destroyed or ended by governments and legal systems that criminalized who they were. Many were brilliant, defiant, and irreplaceable.

Alan Turing
1912 – 1954  ·  Age 41
British mathematician and WWII codebreaker. Convicted of "gross indecency" for homosexuality in 1952. Subjected to chemical castration by court order. Died of cyanide poisoning in 1954. Widely regarded as a persecution-related death. Posthumously pardoned in 2013.
Oscar Wilde
1854 – 1900  ·  Age 46
Irish playwright and poet. Imprisoned 1895–1897 for "gross indecency" for homosexuality. Subjected to hard labor which destroyed his health. Released destitute and in failing health. Died in Paris in 1900. "To deny one's own experiences is to put a lie into the lips of one's own life."
Harvey Milk
1930 – 1978  ·  Age 48
First openly gay person elected to public office in California. San Francisco City Supervisor. Assassinated on November 27, 1978, by former supervisor Dan White. His death sparked the White Night riots when White received a manslaughter sentence.
Federico García Lorca
1898 – 1936  ·  Age 38
Spanish poet and playwright. Executed by Nationalist forces at the start of the Spanish Civil War in August 1936. His homosexuality was among the reasons used to justify his murder. His body was never recovered.
Marsha P. Johnson
1945 – 1992  ·  Age 46
Black trans woman, LGBTQ+ activist, and prominent figure in the 1969 Stonewall uprising. Co-founder of STAR (Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries). Found drowned in the Hudson River on July 6, 1992. Death initially ruled suicide; later changed to undetermined. Many in the community believe she was murdered.
Roger Casement
1864 – 1916  ·  Age 51
Irish human rights activist and diplomat who documented atrocities in the Congo and Peru. Executed for treason in 1916. His private diaries documenting his homosexuality were leaked by the British government to discredit his cause and prevent clemency appeals.
The AIDS Epidemic

Lost to a Crisis Met with Silence

The AIDS epidemic killed hundreds of thousands of gay and queer people in the United States alone — many abandoned by their government, their families, and their institutions. These are some of the artists, activists, and individuals whose names we carry. The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt holds over 110,000 names.

Freddie Mercury
1946 – 1991  ·  Age 45
Musician and lead vocalist of Queen. Died of bronchopneumonia as a complication of AIDS on November 24, 1991 — one day after publicly announcing his diagnosis.
Rock Hudson
1925 – 1985  ·  Age 59
Hollywood actor. The first major celebrity to publicly announce he was dying of AIDS, in July 1985. His death helped push Congress toward increased AIDS research funding.
Keith Haring
1958 – 1990  ·  Age 31
Artist and LGBTQ+ activist. Founded the Keith Haring Foundation in 1989 to support AIDS organizations and children's programs. Died of AIDS-related complications on February 16, 1990.
Robert Mapplethorpe
1946 – 1989  ·  Age 42
Photographer known for his intimate and controversial work. Died of AIDS-related complications on March 9, 1989. His work continued to be censored and fought over long after his death.
Sylvester James
1947 – 1988  ·  Age 41
Openly gay Black disco and R&B singer known as "The Queen of Disco." His falsetto voice and fearless presence made him an icon. Died of AIDS-related heart failure on December 16, 1988. He left his royalties to AIDS charities.
Halston (Roy Frowick)
1932 – 1990  ·  Age 57
American fashion designer who defined 1970s American style. Died of AIDS-related Kaposi's sarcoma on March 26, 1990, in San Francisco.
Perry Ellis
1940 – 1986  ·  Age 46
American fashion designer. Died on May 30, 1986. His cause of death was listed as viral encephalitis; AIDS was not publicly disclosed at the time but is widely acknowledged.
Arthur Russell
1951 – 1992  ·  Age 40
Musician and composer whose work spanned disco, avant-garde, and folk. Died of AIDS-related complications on April 4, 1992. His music was largely rediscovered and celebrated after his death.
David Wojnarowicz
1954 – 1992  ·  Age 37
Artist, writer, and AIDS activist. A fierce voice against government inaction during the epidemic. Died of AIDS-related complications on July 22, 1992. His words: "If I die of AIDS, forget burial — just drop my body on the steps of the FDA."
Essex Hemphill
1957 – 1995  ·  Age 38
Black gay poet and activist. Editor of "Brother to Brother: New Writings by Black Gay Men." Died of AIDS-related complications on November 4, 1995.
Paul Monette
1945 – 1995  ·  Age 49
Poet and memoirist. Author of "Borrowed Time: An AIDS Memoir" and "Becoming a Man," which won the National Book Award. Died of AIDS-related complications on February 10, 1995.
Cookie Mueller
1949 – 1989  ·  Age 40
Writer, actress, and downtown New York fixture who appeared in many of John Waters' films. Died of AIDS-related complications on November 10, 1989.
Hate Crimes & Violence

Murdered for Who They Were

People killed because of their sexual orientation or gender identity. These are documented hate crimes and targeted killings. They represent thousands more tracked by the Transgender Day of Remembrance and the Human Rights Campaign.

Matthew Shepard
1976 – 1998  ·  Age 21
Gay student at the University of Wyoming. Tied to a fence post and beaten by two men in Laramie, Wyoming, on October 7, 1998. Died on October 12, 1998. His death led to the federal Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act, signed in 2009.
Brandon Teena
1972 – 1993  ·  Age 21
Trans man. Raped and then murdered in Falls City, Nebraska on December 31, 1993 by two men after his trans identity was revealed. His story is told in the film "Boys Don't Cry" (1999).
Gwen Araujo
1985 – 2002  ·  Age 17
Trans Latina woman. Murdered by four men in Newark, California on October 3, 2002 after they discovered she was trans. Her murder prompted California to pass legislation limiting the use of the trans panic defense.
Sakia Gunn
1987 – 2003  ·  Age 15
Black lesbian teenager. Stabbed while waiting for a bus with friends in Newark, New Jersey on May 11, 2003, after identifying herself as gay when two men made advances. One of the youngest known victims of an anti-LGBTQ+ hate crime.
Lawrence "Larry" King
1993 – 2008  ·  Age 15
Gay and gender nonconforming student at E.O. Green Junior High School in Oxnard, California. Shot twice in the back of the head by a 14-year-old classmate on February 12, 2008. He died two days later.
Angie Zapata
1989 – 2008  ·  Age 18
Trans woman. Murdered in Greeley, Colorado in July 2008. Her killer became the first person in the United States convicted under a hate crime law for killing a transgender person.
Duanna Johnson
1965 – 2008  ·  Age 43
Black trans woman. Beaten by Memphis police officers in a booking room in February 2008 — a beating caught on video. Shot and killed in November 2008. Her case illustrated the compounding vulnerability of trans women of color to both police violence and street violence.
Jorge Steven Lopez Mercado
1992 – 2009  ·  Age 17
Gay Puerto Rican youth. Murdered near Cayey, Puerto Rico in November 2009. His body was dismembered and partially burned. His death prompted widespread protests demanding justice for LGBTQ+ Puerto Ricans.
Mark Carson
1981 – 2013  ·  Age 32
Black gay man. Shot point-blank in Greenwich Village, New York City on May 18, 2013 by a man who had been following and taunting him and his partner with anti-gay slurs. His murder galvanized the New York LGBTQ+ community.
Islan Nettles
1993 – 2013  ·  Age 21
Black trans woman. Beaten to death in Harlem, New York in August 2013. Her killer discovered she was trans and beat her while bystanders watched. Her case became a rallying point for justice for trans women of color.
Brianna Ghey
2006 – 2023  ·  Age 16
Trans girl from Birchwood, Warrington, England. Murdered on February 11, 2023, in a planned attack by two teenagers. Her mother, Esther Ghey, became a prominent advocate for online safety and anti-knife legislation.
Nex Benedict
2007 – 2024  ·  Age 16
Nonbinary (they/he) student in Owasso, Oklahoma. Beaten in a school bathroom by multiple students on February 7, 2024. Died the following day. Their death drew national attention to anti-LGBTQ+ legislation targeting youth in Oklahoma and across the country.
Transgender Day of Remembrance — Selected Names

Trans Lives Lost to Violence

The Transgender Day of Remembrance (TDOR), founded in 1999 by Gwendolyn Ann Smith, memorializes trans people killed due to anti-transgender violence. Trans women of color are disproportionately represented among victims. The names below are drawn from HRC's annual fatal violence tracking. The full TDOR list documents hundreds per year — thousands since 1999.

The Full Record

The names above represent a small portion of those documented. These organizations maintain comprehensive, ongoing records — together, they hold thousands of names and the evidence of a systemic crisis.

↗
Transgender Day of Remembrance
Founded 1999 by Gwendolyn Ann Smith. Documents trans people killed due to anti-trans violence worldwide, annually. Hundreds of names per year — thousands total.
 ↗
HRC — Fatal Anti-Transgender Violence
Annual Human Rights Campaign report documenting fatal violence against trans and gender nonconforming people in the United States, with individual names and circumstances.
 ↗
NAMES Project — AIDS Memorial Quilt
Over 110,000 names of people lost to AIDS/HIV, displayed on hand-stitched panels. The largest piece of community folk art in the world. Searchable database available online.
 ↗
Anti-Violence Project
Documents and responds to violence against LGBTQ+ and HIV-affected communities. Maintains incident data and provides direct services to survivors.
 ↗
GLAAD — TDOR Resources
Annual Transgender Day of Remembrance resources and coverage, including context on the epidemic of anti-trans violence.
 ↗
Matthew Shepard Foundation
Founded by Dennis and Judy Shepard after the murder of their son Matthew in 1998. Works to replace hate with understanding, compassion, and acceptance.
